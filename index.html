Systematic withdrawal plan

A rooftop water tank with a tap.

That is how you get a steady paycheck from money you already saved.

The picture

The tank holds your savings. The tap lets out a fixed amount each month. The bucket is your pay.

Three moves

Fill the tank

Put a lump of money into an investment pot. This is a big shared pot, run by professionals.

Set the tap

Pick an amount and a day. Say Rs 8,000 on the 1st.

Let it rain

What is left in the tank keeps growing, or shrinking, with the market.

Try the tap

Change the numbers. Watch how long the tank lasts. These are example figures, not a forecast.

How the math works: rain is a true yearly return (10% means the tank grows exactly 10% over a year), split into equal monthly steps. The tap runs at the end of each month. Rain is assumed to be the same every year. Real markets are bumpy, which is exactly the risk below.

Where the tank can run dry

A dry spell early

If the market falls in the first years, the tap still pours the same amount. The tank drains faster, and a later recovery has less water to work with.

Tap bigger than rain

If you take out more than the tank earns, you are eating the tank itself. It will empty. Only the date is in question.

The taxman

Each drop that leaves may count as a sale. Gains can be taxed. Check your country's rules and whether your pot offers this plan at all.

A withdrawal plan turns a lump of savings into a steady monthly paycheck, and it lasts only as long as the tap stays smaller than the rain.
